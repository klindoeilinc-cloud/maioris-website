The cost of a vague brief: why creative inefficiency starts before production even begins

A disappointing creative result is not always the consequence of poor execution. The problem often starts much earlier, with a brief that fails to define the problem, the objective or the decision framework clearly.

MAIORIS Insights21 November 20263 min read

Creative inefficiency rarely starts where organisations expect to find it.

It is tempting to blame delays, execution inconsistencies or disappointing results on production teams, agencies or creative talent.

Yet in many situations the problem appears much earlier: when the brief is written.

A brief is not simply a document describing what to produce. It is a strategic instrument that translates a business challenge into clear creative direction.

When that translation fails, the whole creative process becomes vulnerable.

The invisible cost of ambiguity

An imprecise brief creates uncertainty across the entire creative ecosystem.

Teams interpret objectives differently. Agencies form their own hypotheses. Stakeholders introduce contradictory expectations. Feedback becomes subjective and approval cycles get complicated.

The result is an accumulation of dysfunctions:

  • Repeated revisions.
  • Longer production timelines.
  • Misaligned expectations.
  • Additional coordination costs.
  • Output disconnected from business priorities.

The organisation believes it is investing more in its creative activity.

In reality, it is sometimes paying several times over for a problem that was never properly defined.

From a production request to a strategic brief

An effective brief must establish five fundamental elements:

  1. The business problem: what really needs to change?
  2. The audience: which perception, behaviour or decision are we trying to influence?
  3. The objective: what result should the creative intervention produce?
  4. The strategic direction: which proposition or idea should guide the work?
  5. The success criteria: how will the effectiveness of the result be assessed?

These elements turn the brief into a genuine decision framework.

The organisational dimension

Improving briefs is not only about writing better documents.

It requires clearer leadership, better defined responsibilities and a shared understanding of the organisation's expectations.

This is where creative governance becomes essential.

When objectives, responsibilities and evaluation criteria are set before execution, teams can work with more clarity and fewer pointless iterations.

The MAIORIS perspective

At MAIORIS, we see the brief as one of the first control points of a creative performance system.

A creative system cannot produce lasting value when its inputs remain ambiguous.

The aim is not to limit creative exploration.

It is to create the conditions in which that exploration can produce relevant results.

The real question is not whether your teams produce enough creative content. It is whether they are solving the right problem from the start.
