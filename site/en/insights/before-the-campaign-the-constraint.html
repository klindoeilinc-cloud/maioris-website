Before the campaign, find the constraint.

Most briefs ask for visibility. Many problems are not visibility problems. Why diagnosis has to come before creation.

Marko Leo19 November 20264 min read

A brief usually arrives with its answer already written: a campaign, a launch, more content. The organisation has seen a symptom (sales that stall, an app that is downloaded but not used, a brand that no longer stands out) and it has chosen the most familiar remedy.

The problem is that a symptom can have very different causes. A product that does not keep its promise, a distribution network that does not follow, a customer service that loses trust, a sign-up journey that discourages: none of these is solved by a better campaign. A strong campaign can even make things worse, by sending more people towards the point that is failing.

One constraint at a time

The Theory of Constraints starts from a simple idea: in any system, one bottleneck limits the whole. Improving everything else changes almost nothing as long as that point is not released. Applied to brands, it means asking a different first question. Not "what should we say?", but "what is really preventing the objective from being reached?".

A campaign is an answer. It is worth something only if the question was the right one.

Three questions before any brief

  • Where exactly does the journey break? Awareness, consideration, first purchase, repeat purchase or recommendation.
  • Which data shows it, and which data is missing? An assumption written down is worth more than a certainty repeated.
  • If the creative work succeeds perfectly, what will still block the result?

These questions take days, not months. They often change the brief, sometimes the budget, and occasionally the decision to communicate at all. That is precisely their value: they protect the creative investment instead of exposing it.

This is why MCPS begins with Observe, Diagnose and Prioritise, and why no creative territory is designed before the main constraint has been validated. Intelligence precedes execution, not as a slogan, but as an order of operations.
