How do you measure the real return on creative investment?

Creative quality matters. But organisations must also understand how their creative investment contributes to brand strength, operational efficiency and business performance.

MAIORIS Insights21 November 20263 min read

Creativity is one of the most visible investments a brand makes.

Campaigns, visual identities, content platforms and brand experiences are regularly assessed through presentations, internal feedback and aesthetic judgement.

But one essential question remains:

How can an organisation tell whether its creative investment really produces value?

The limits of conventional evaluation

Many organisations assess their creative output using indicators such as:

  • The number of pieces of content produced.
  • Campaign reach.
  • Engagement.
  • Impressions.
  • Production costs.

These indicators are useful, but they do not tell the whole story.

High engagement does not automatically translate into commercial performance.

A large volume of content does not necessarily mean better differentiation.

And a visually remarkable campaign does not guarantee that the original business problem has been solved.

A broader performance framework

Creative performance should be examined along four dimensions.

1. Strategic effectiveness

Does the creative intervention really answer the brand or business problem that was identified?

2. Brand effectiveness

Does it help strengthen recognition, differentiation, relevance or brand consistency?

3. Operational effectiveness

Are resources used efficiently? Do approval times, production processes and collaboration mechanisms work properly?

4. Business contribution

Are there measurable elements that make it possible to assess its contribution to qualified demand, conversion, acquisition, retention or revenue?

Not every creative initiative directly influences all of these indicators.

The measurement framework must be adapted to the objective of each intervention.

Building measurement in from the start

One of the most common mistakes is to wait until the end of a campaign to decide how to evaluate its results.

Performance criteria must be defined before execution.

This means linking the initial brief to measurable objectives, setting the relevant indicators and identifying the data needed for evaluation.

It also means distinguishing between deliverables produced, results obtained and longer term effects.

The MAIORIS perspective

Creative performance is not simply about producing more with fewer resources.

It is about establishing better alignment between strategic intent, creative investment, operational execution and measurable value.

This requires a genuine evaluation system rather than isolated performance reports.

The best creative investment is not necessarily the one that produces the most deliverables. It is the one that creates the most value in relation to the problem it was meant to solve.
