Intelligence System

Intelligence before execution. Strategy before production. Systems for repeatable performance.

MCPS (MAIORIS Creative Performance System) is the method behind every MAIORIS engagement. It connects creative intelligence, strategy, systems, AI and measurement into one cycle.

The cycle

Seven steps, three human decision gates.

  1. ObserveUnderstand context, data, behaviours and constraints.
  2. DiagnoseFind the real problem and the main bottleneck.
  3. PrioritiseChoose the interventions with the highest value.
  4. DesignBuild the strategic, creative and operational response.
  5. GovernSet responsibilities, approvals and decision rules.
  6. ExecuteDeliver with teams, partners and tools.
  7. Measure & improveTrack gaps, learn, and improve continuously.

The six stages

What MCPS covers.

A strategic operating system designed to transform creative intelligence into structured decisions, operational coherence and measurable creative performance.

MCPS is not a service in the catalogue: it is the proprietary methodology that informs every service. The six stages describe what it covers; the seven step cycle above describes how each engagement is run.

1 · Creative Intelligence

PurposeGround creative decisions in a documented understanding of market, audience and competition.

Strategic questionWhat do we actually know, and what do we only assume?

Type of interventionResearch and analysis that separate facts, observations, hypotheses and unknowns.

Expected outputA documented reading of the context, with each statement carrying its status.

Observable evidenceSources are cited; facts can be told apart from interpretation.

2 · Creative Strategy

PurposeTurn understanding into choices: positioning, priorities and creative direction aligned with the business.

Strategic questionWhat do we choose, and what do we leave aside?

Type of interventionStrategic decisions, prioritisation and direction, taken with the leadership team.

Expected outputWritten strategic choices and priorities.

Observable evidenceDecisions can be traced back to the intelligence, with the trade offs stated.

3 · Creative System

PurposeDesign the organisation that carries the strategy: roles, flows and resources.

Strategic questionHow must the creative function be built to carry this strategy?

Type of interventionOrganisational design and workflow architecture.

Expected outputA defined creative architecture: roles, responsibilities, flows and resource allocation.

Observable evidenceRoles and flows are documented; each has a named owner.

4 · Creative Operating System

PurposeMake the way decisions are taken and work is validated explicit and repeatable.

Strategic questionWho decides what, on which criteria, and how does work move?

Type of interventionGovernance, briefing and approval design, with the teams concerned.

Expected outputGovernance rules, briefing and approval processes, decision rights.

Observable evidenceApprovals follow stated criteria; decisions are recorded.

5 · AI Creative Infrastructure

PurposeUse AI to augment capabilities and improve workflows, under human control.

Strategic questionWhere does AI help, and who remains accountable?

Type of interventionOpportunity mapping and design of governed, AI assisted workflows.

Expected outputA classification of tasks (automatable, AI assisted, human expertise, mandatory approval) and the rules around it.

Observable evidenceEvery AI use has a named human owner and a quality check.

6 · Creative Performance

PurposeKnow whether the system works, and adapt it.

Strategic questionHow do we know it works, and what do we change next?

Type of interventionIndicator design and a review rhythm.

Expected outputIndicators defined before the work starts, and a routine to review them.

Observable evidenceIndicators are tracked over time against targets set beforehand.

Philosophy

A chain, not a menu.

Creative Intelligence → Creative Strategy → Creative System → Creative Operating System → AI Creative Infrastructure → Creative Performance

AI and data

AI is infrastructure, not identity.

We use AI where it measurably saves time or improves quality, and never without a human owner for the decision. Every task is classified: automatable, AI-assisted, human expertise, or mandatory approval.

Every important statement in our work carries its status (verified fact, observation, estimate, hypothesis or unknown) so that leaders know what they are deciding on.
