Know what your brand really means, then decide what it should mean.

An evidence-based reading of your brand, your audiences and your competitive territory, leading to a clear and defensible positioning.

§ 01 · Business challenge

The brand is visible, but not distinct.

Campaigns succeed one another without building a lasting meaning. Positioning lives in a document that teams and agencies interpret differently.

§ 02 · Strategic opportunity

A positioning that guides every creative decision.

When the brand's territory is clear and grounded in evidence, briefs become sharper, approvals faster and campaigns cumulative.

§ 03 · Methodology

Observe

Audit of brand assets, audience and competitor review, cultural signals.

Diagnose

What the brand owns, borrows and lacks.

Design

Positioning, brand platform and strategic narrative.

§ 04 · Scope

Brand, sub-brands, audiences, competitors and communication history.

Not included: creative production and media buying.

§ 05 · Deliverables

  • Brand intelligence report
  • Positioning and brand platform
  • Strategic narrative
  • Creative direction principles

§ 06 · Engagement model

  • Defined project
  • Run end to end by senior leadership
  • Can extend into Growth Architecture

§ 07 · Expected outcomes

  • A positioning your leadership can defend.
  • Briefs that agencies interpret the same way.
  • A basis for measuring brand progress.

§ 08 · Why this offer

Brand Intelligence develops the strategic understanding and architecture of a brand. Growth Architecture structures growth opportunities and business priorities. Campaign Architect designs the architecture of a high value campaign. They are three distinct interventions, and their scopes are not merged.
